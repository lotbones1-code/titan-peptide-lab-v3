# Paid Ads Compliance Guide — Peptide Research Chemicals

## The Core Challenge

Peptide products sit in a gray area for ad platforms. They're legal research
chemicals, but platforms often flag them alongside pharmaceuticals and
supplements. The goal: position Titan as a laboratory supplier, not a
health/supplement company.

---

## Google Ads Policy

### What Gets Flagged

Google's **Healthcare and medicines** policy restricts:
- Prescription pharmaceuticals
- Unapproved pharmaceutical supplements
- Products making therapeutic claims

Peptide research chemicals are NOT pharmaceuticals, but automated review
systems sometimes flag them. Key triggers:

1. **Health claims** — "heals tissue", "reduces anxiety", "improves sleep"
2. **Dosing instructions** — "take 2 sprays daily"
3. **Before/after language** — implying human therapeutic outcomes
4. **Pharmaceutical terms** — "drug", "medication", "treatment", "therapy"

### How to Stay Approved

**DO:**
- Use "research compound" / "research chemical" / "laboratory peptide"
- Say "researchers report" not "users experience"
- Frame everything as lab/research use
- Include "for research purposes only" in ad copy or landing page
- Emphasize purity, COA, HPLC — supply chain language
- Use product/compound names (BPC-157, Selank) — these are chemical names, not drug names

**DON'T:**
- Make any health, therapeutic, or medical claims
- Use words: treatment, cure, heal, therapy, dose (use "measured spray" instead)
- Imply human consumption or personal use
- Show humans using the product
- Reference FDA approval or clinical trials as endorsement
- Use testimonials about health outcomes

### Ad Copy Patterns That Pass Review

```
SAFE: "BPC-157 nasal spray — 500mcg per spray, in-house HPLC ≥99% release target"
RISK: "BPC-157 spray for tissue healing and gut repair"

SAFE: "Research-grade peptide with lot-matched COA"
RISK: "Peptide supplement for recovery and performance"

SAFE: "Cited in angiogenesis, GI-mucosa, and inflammatory-pathway research literature"
RISK: "Researchers report systemic anti-inflammatory action" (still implies a real effect)
RISK: "Reduces inflammation and speeds up healing"

SAFE: "Selank — GABA and serotonin pathway research"
RISK: "Selank — reduces anxiety and improves mood"
```

### If an Ad Gets Disapproved

1. Check the specific policy violation in the Google Ads dashboard
2. Don't appeal immediately — rewrite the ad to be more compliant first
3. If "Healthcare and medicines" — remove any language implying human use
4. If "Misrepresentation" — ensure landing page matches ad claims exactly
5. Appeal with a note: "This is a laboratory research chemical supplier, not a pharmaceutical or supplement company. Products are sold for research purposes only."

### Landing Page Requirements

Google checks landing pages too. Ensure:
- "For research purposes only" disclaimer is visible
- No health claims on the landing page
- Clear business information (name, contact)
- Privacy policy and terms of service accessible
- No misleading pricing or fake urgency

The Titan site already has the research disclaimer in the footer. Make sure it's
also visible above the fold on any dedicated landing page.

## Meta (Facebook/Instagram) Policy

### What Gets Flagged

Meta's **Drugs and drug-related products** and **Unsafe supplements** policies:
- Ads must not promote the sale or use of illegal, prescription, or recreational drugs
- Ads must not promote unsafe supplements (as determined by Meta)

Peptide sprays can trigger automated review under "unsafe supplements."

### How to Stay Approved

**DO:**
- Position as laboratory/research supply company
- Focus on quality (purity testing, COA, cold-chain)
- Use scientific/chemical language
- Target audience interests in research, science, pharmacology
- Keep landing pages clinical and professional

**DON'T:**
- Show product being applied to human body
- Use "supplement" anywhere
- Make before/after claims
- Use language implying personal consumption
- Target broad health/wellness audiences (triggers manual review)

### Meta-Specific Creative Rules

1. **No before/after images** — even implied
2. **No body-focused imagery** — no muscles, skin close-ups, human faces in pain/relief
3. **Text on images < 20%** — Meta penalizes text-heavy images
4. **No emojis suggesting outcomes** — no 💪 🧠 😴 in ad copy
5. **Landing page must match** — if ad says "research grade", landing page can't say "for your health"

### Meta Approval Strategy

1. Start with retargeting campaigns (warmer audience, lower scrutiny)
2. Use the most conservative copy first — establish account history
3. Gradually introduce more specific compound language
4. If flagged, simplify copy to pure supply/quality messaging
5. Appeal with: "We are a laboratory supply company selling research chemicals. Products are clearly marked 'for research purposes only' on our website."

## Compliance Checklist — Before Launching Any Ad

- [ ] No health, therapeutic, or medical claims in ad copy
- [ ] No words: treatment, cure, heal, therapy, supplement, dose
- [ ] "Research" or "laboratory" framing used throughout
- [ ] No images of humans using products
- [ ] Landing page has "for research purposes only" disclaimer visible
- [ ] Landing page has privacy policy and terms links
- [ ] Price matches between ad and landing page
- [ ] No fake urgency or scarcity (unless genuine limited stock)
- [ ] Discount codes are real and working
- [ ] UTM parameters are appended to all URLs
- [ ] Creative meets platform-specific requirements (size, text ratio)

## Platform-Specific Account Setup Notes

### Google Ads

- Apply for **Pharmacy certification** ONLY if you want to run
  pharmaceutical ads (you probably don't — it's a different category)
- Do NOT check "healthcare" as your business category during setup
- Set business category to: "Science & Technology" or "Retail"
- Enable Enhanced Conversions for better attribution

### Meta Ads

- Verify your domain (titanpeptidelab.com) in Business Manager
- Do NOT select "Health & Wellness" as your ad category
- Install Conversions API (server-side) for reliable tracking
- Set up Aggregated Event Measurement (AEM) with events in priority:
  1. Purchase
  2. InitiateCheckout
  3. AddToCart
  4. ViewContent

## Worst-Case Scenarios

### Account suspension (Google or Meta)

1. Do NOT create a new account (they'll ban it too)
2. File an appeal explaining: laboratory research chemical supplier, for research only
3. Include screenshots of your research disclaimer, COA page, terms of service
4. If denied, request manual review with additional documentation
5. If permanently banned, explore alternative channels: Reddit Ads, Programmatic Display, SEO

### Individual ad disapproval

1. Don't appeal the same ad — rewrite it
2. Make it MORE conservative, not less
3. Re-submit and wait 24–48 hours
4. If still disapproved, that angle may not be viable on that platform

### Policy team reach-out

1. Respond promptly and professionally
2. Provide all documentation requested
3. Emphasize: legal product, research use only, clear disclaimers
4. Offer to modify any specific ad or landing page content
5. Ask for specific guidance on what would be acceptable

## Alternative Channels If Platforms Restrict

If Google/Meta prove too restrictive for peptide ads:

1. **Reddit Ads** — research/lab-oriented communities (e.g. r/peptides) with research-use framing; avoid self-use/biohacking audiences.
2. **Programmatic Display** — via platforms like StackAdapt, AdRoll
3. **Newsletter Sponsorships** — research-chemistry / lab-supply newsletters; avoid biohacking/self-optimization lists.
4. **Trade/Science Channels** — research-supply directories and lab-procurement contexts.
5. **SEO + Content** — long-term organic strategy (most sustainable)
6. **Affiliate/Referral** — partner with researchers and review sites
7. **Twitter/X Ads** — generally more permissive on research chemicals
